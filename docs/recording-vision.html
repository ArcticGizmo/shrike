Shrike/Recording · Narration Vision — not yet locked

Talk over your captures without the re-take dread.

Narration in Shrike is a track, not something welded to the take. Record it live while you demo, or lay it down in the editor against the preview — and when you fumble a line, re-record just that slice. It rides the cuts like every other effect. But first, we settle the thing that always goes wrong: is the right mic actually armed and audible?

🪶

Shrike's edit model is already non-destructive and source-time-anchored, so audio drops in as an editable layer that survives trims and splits for free. The same move opens the door to burned-in captions for the muted-autoplay Slack case investigate later — that's on the roadmap, not in this vision.

01

The mic check comes before the record light the pain point

You can't un-fumble a take because the wrong input was armed, the mic was muted, or the level was pinned. So recording is gated on a check: pick the device, watch it move, record a three-second test and hear it back. Only then does the record button light up.

1
Device picker, remembered

The last-used input is pre-armed next time. USB mic unplugged? It says so instead of silently falling back to the laptop array.

2
A meter that actually moves

Live input level with a peak marker. You see the mic respond to your voice — proof the device is live and roughly set before you commit.

3
Test & play back

Records three seconds and plays them straight back. The one check that catches muted mics, wrong device, and “why am I so quiet” — every time.

4
Record stays dim until it's happy

No signal, no arm. The button won't pretend you're ready when the input is silent.

5
Mic, system sound, or both

Flip on loopback to capture what the machine is playing — app audio, a video, a chime. Each source is its own track, so gains stay independent in the editor.

02

Record with your voice on the same clock

The HUD keeps the mic in view the whole take — a live meter so you never trail off into a dead channel, plus the armed device by name. Mic audio is captured on the same pause-excluded clock as the pointer track, so it lines up with the video and the click ripples automatically. Want the app's own sound too? Loopback capture is a toggle, mixed underneath.

03

Re-record the fumble, keep the rest

In the editor the narration is a waveform lane right under the filmstrip. Prefer to narrate after the fact? Hit record in the editor and talk over the playing preview. Flubbed one sentence? Select the span, punch in, and re-record only that slice — everything either side stays put. And because Shrike is WYSIWYG, the preview plays the narration back as you scrub, so what you hear is what you'll ship.

04

Export mixes it in — off still means off

When there's a narration track, the encode stops forcing -an and muxes the audio into the same single decode → compose → encode pass. The live size estimate now counts an audio stream. No narration? The export path is byte-for-byte what it is today.

◆

Held to the project's rules

WYSIWYG

Audible in export ⇒ audible in preview. The PreviewSurface plays the narration as you scrub.

Off means off

No audio track ⇒ export is byte-for-byte today's transcode, still -an.

Non-destructive

Audio is sidecar media referenced from *.edit.json (v3). The source MP4 is never mutated.

Core stays UI-free

Capture, mixing, level metering land in Shrike.Core with headless tests.

Snappy load stays green

Nothing audio touches tray start. No models, no device enumeration until you open recording.

One open decision

Audio + preview playback is the moment to unify the dual-path preview — or defer it once more.
